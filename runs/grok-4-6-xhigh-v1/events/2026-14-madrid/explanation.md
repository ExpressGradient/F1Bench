## Championship and form

After 13 races (Monza): Antonelli 267, Russell 201, Hamilton 191, Norris 171, Leclerc 155, Verstappen 127, Piastri 116. Mercedes 468–346 over Ferrari in constructors.

Antonelli has seven wins including a 19th-to-first recovery at Monza last weekend. Mercedes swept practice here (Russell FP1, Antonelli FP2/FP3). Norris has won two of the last three Grands Prix (Hungary, Zandvoort) and produced what he called one of his best laps for pole. Ferrari have been Mercedes’ closest match on Friday/Saturday long-run and one-lap pace. Red Bull are a step behind the top three teams; Verstappen still extracted P3. Alpine (Mercedes PU) and Racing Bulls occupy the real midfield; Audi/Haas next; Williams off the pace; Aston Martin’s Honda package has been a reliability and speed disaster; Cadillac (Ferrari customer PU) has 0 points in 13 races.

## Circuit and race character

Madring is a first-time hybrid street/permanent layout: 22 corners, ~340 km/h on the straights, 24% banked “La Monumental”. F3 testing produced 19 red flags. Verstappen and Williams called it a potential “car killer”. Drivers (Antonelli, Russell, Hamilton, Alpine) all said on-track passing is painful — Antonelli spent two practice laps stuck behind a Williams. The run to Turn 1 is only ~200 m, so the start is bunched rather than a long drag.

Weather: dry, air high-20s/low-30s °C, track into the 50s. No rain. Pirelli C2/C3/C4; one-stop medium–hard is the natural call because pit loss plus hard passing punish an extra stop, though Friday graining and a two-stop (M–H–H) remain live. Norris/Antonelli/Verstappen/Russell/Piastri have no new softs; Hamilton and Leclerc kept a fresh C4 as a start/undercut wildcard. Safety-car market was heavily “yes” (~73%).

## Model

120k-race Monte Carlo with:

- Qualifying grid after penalties, plus a small T1 launch mix (Ferrari’s new-soft option, Verstappen’s starts, short run to T1).
- Two finishing-order regimes: a **processional** Plackett–Luce (track position dominates, matching driver feedback) and a **chaos** regime (multiple SCs / red flag / pit lottery) in which Mercedes race pace can still come through, as at Monza.
- Driver-specific mechanical and wall-incident hazards, elevated versus a mature permanent circuit. Extra DNS risk for Bearman (impact-structure rebuild) and Stroll (fresh Honda PU that already leaked water). Small DSQ risk, slightly higher at the front because of plank/skid wear on the banking.
- 90% rule: most DNFs are NC; a minority of late stoppages still classify.

Win probabilities are close to the post-quali market (Antonelli ≈ Norris, then Verstappen, then the Ferraris) but give Russell more win chance than the +2200 board, because the W17 is still the best race car and one SC cycle is enough to use it.
